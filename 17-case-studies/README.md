# Case Studies

**→ [The case studies](CASE-STUDIES.md)** — 10 systems, 2007 to 2023, every one with a public
primary source: a paper, an official engineering blog, or a published engineering guide.

These are the systems everyone cites and almost nobody reads. That gap is where the damage happens.
An architecture turns up in a design review with a famous company's name attached and none of its
constraints attached — and the constraints were the entire reason it looked like that.

So each case here is written to answer three questions rather than one:

- **What could they not change?** A design is only interesting relative to its constraints. Google
  could put atomic clocks in the rack. You cannot, and that is not a detail.
- **What did each decision cost?** A decision without a cost was not a decision, it was a
  preference. Every decision on the page carries its bill.
- **When would copying this be wrong?** The honest half, stated per case. Most readers operate
  three or four orders of magnitude below these systems, and at that scale several of these designs
  are straightforwardly the wrong answer.

## How to use it

Read the **surprise** and the **when copying it would be wrong** sections first. The architecture
diagrams are the part you already half-know; those two are the part that changes what you do on
Monday.

If you are preparing for an interview, the useful move is to be able to state one of these as a
chain — the pressure, the change, the cost — in about ninety seconds. Naming a system is worth
nothing; naming what it gave up is worth a great deal.

## What is deliberately not here

Systems without a public primary source. There is a large secondary literature of conference-talk
summaries and blog posts describing what a company *probably* does, and it is where most invented
numbers come from. If a figure could not be traced to a paper, an official engineering blog or
published vendor guidance, it is described qualitatively instead or left out.

Also absent: any case whose lesson is only *they made it bigger*. A case earns a place by being
counterintuitive, not by being large.

## Generated

`CASE-STUDIES.md` and `cases.json` are generated by
[`scripts/gen_case_studies.py`](../scripts/gen_case_studies.py) from
[`scripts/case_study_data.py`](../scripts/case_study_data.py); CI fails if the committed files drift
from the data. Edit the data, not the markdown.

The generator also enforces the things that decay first: a `doesNotApply` shrinking to a
disclaimer, a decision whose cost has gone missing, a diagram colour outside the palette, and a
`seeAlso` pointing at a page that has since been renamed.
